# Reading report — DEA 1994

This document records a complete first-pass rereading of `old/DEA-1994.txt`. It replaces the preliminary checkpoint notes written before the full text had been read.

The purpose is not to judge a 1994 DEA by present-day standards, nor to retrofit later work into it. The aim is to reconstruct what the thesis actually attempted, distinguish results from intuitions and open questions, identify limitations due to formal background or computing resources, and isolate ideas that may deserve a modern reformulation.

## 1. Overall reading

The thesis is broader than the current project's shorthand description as a "static sieve". Its central problem is how to *explore* cellular computation when both relevant spaces are combinatorially huge:

- the space of local transition rules;
- the space of configurations and trajectories generated by each rule.

The response is deliberately experimental and constructive. Rather than beginning with a mathematical classification, the thesis tries to build a workbench in which rules, spaces, time sequences, reductions and visualizations can be manipulated as objects. Fast cellular engines, lookup-table representations, sequencing, image-processing tools, reductions, projections and rule-space parameters are parts of one exploration strategy.

This makes the thesis less a single theorem or algorithm than an attempt to construct an *experimental language* for cellular automata.

Three scientific threads are especially visible:

1. representation and efficient execution of cellular rules;
2. reduction and visualization of high-dimensional dynamics;
3. structural exploration of rule space, culminating in the embedding/influence parameter.

A fourth thread, the experimental study of Anneal, connects the tooling back to dynamical questions.

## 2. The conceptual architecture: space, time and laws

Chapter 2 organizes the work around three elementary objects: **space**, **time**, and **laws** (transition functions). This organization is more durable than much of the implementation around it.

Space is not merely an array on which a CA runs. It is an object supporting editing, reduction, transformation and visualization. The text repeatedly treats loss of information as an intentional experimental operation: populations, differential reductions, slices, projections and texture measurements are ways of mapping an inaccessible state space into observable quantities.

Time is similarly treated as an object rather than just an iteration counter. A trajectory can be transformed, reduced, sliced, accumulated or viewed as an additional spatial dimension. This leads naturally to space-time representations and to the idea that the same operators can sometimes act on spatial and temporal dimensions.

The transition law is represented primarily as a lookup table plus a neighborhood/interconnection specification. This representation gives the implementation a useful plasticity: rules can be built, composed, mixed, separated, rebound to different arguments, or generated from higher-level descriptions.

This chapter contains a recurring intuition that is worth retaining: **representation determines which experiments become cheap enough to think about**. The table representation is not presented merely as an optimization. It is also a way to make rule manipulation itself an experimental object.

## 3. What is contingent on 1994 hardware, and what is not

A large fraction of the engineering discussion is necessarily tied to the machines of the period: memory measured in tens of megabytes, cache and paging behavior, bit planes, PBM/PGM pipelines, Tcl/Tk, Khoros, GAPP-like SIMD architectures, and hand-designed lookup-table engines.

Those implementation choices should not be mistaken for the scientific core. However, the resource reasoning behind them remains relevant. The thesis continuously trades:

- memory against CPU;
- preprocessing against repeated execution;
- generality against throughput;
- local detail against reduced observables;
- exhaustive exploration against structured sampling.

Those are exactly the trade-offs faced by the current project, although the numerical thresholds have moved enormously.

The GAPP exercise is therefore historically contingent in its details but conceptually revealing. A processor element is compiled into a lookup table, and arbitrary tables are considered in relation to instruction sequences. This is an exploration of duality between algorithmic and tabular descriptions. The conclusion returns to this issue by presenting `xu`, a rule whose natural implementation is algorithmic and whose state is too structured for the generalized-table approach to remain attractive.

The thesis itself therefore recognizes the limit of its own table-centric bottom-up programme.

## 4. Rule-space structure: the actual problem behind the embedding parameter

Chapter 3 states the problem clearly: qualitative dynamical classes are useful only if one can understand how rules producing them are distributed in rule space. For nontrivial neighborhoods, enumeration rapidly becomes impossible, so one needs control parameters and structured sampling.

The discussion starts from Wolfram's classes and Langton's lambda parameter. Lambda measures the fraction of table entries not mapped to the quiescent state. The thesis emphasizes two limitations:

- nearby lambda values do not uniquely determine nearby rules or similar dynamics;
- the distribution of lambda values over rule space is itself highly non-uniform, so sampling by lambda has a geometry that must be taken into account.

The text is appropriately cautious about the stronger "edge of chaos" claims already being debated in the literature at the time. It cites the Packard/Mitchell disagreement as evidence that the concepts and control parameters are insufficiently understood.

The important move is therefore not "replace lambda by one better scalar". The thesis explicitly says that it seems unlikely that a single additional parameter will suffice.

## 5. Flip, embedding and influence

Before defining embedding, the thesis introduces **Flip**: the normalized number of output-state changes encountered while scanning consecutive entries of a transition table. This is partly motivated by the observation that embedding a smaller-neighborhood binary rule into a larger-neighborhood rule duplicates table entries. Moving away from the embedded subspace can then be performed by flipping selected entries, possibly while keeping lambda constant.

This leads to the stronger construction.

A rule using a neighborhood variable only nominally is really a rule on a smaller effective neighborhood. In the binary case, fixing all other input bits and comparing the two table entries that differ only in one neighborhood bit directly measures whether that bit influences the output.

The thesis defines, for each neighborhood bit, a component of an **embedding/influence vector** `E`. Conceptually, each component is the fraction (or normalized amount) of paired table entries for which changing that particular input changes the output. A zero component means that the corresponding neighbor is irrelevant: the rule lies in an embedded lower-dimensional/lower-neighborhood rule space (or is anisotropic with respect to the nominal neighborhood).

This point corrects the oversimplification in our earlier discussion: **embedding is not fundamentally a scalar parameter**. The primary object is a vector with one component per neighborhood position. The arithmetic mean is proposed only as one possible scalar control parameter analogous to lambda. The thesis explicitly notes that other reductions could be chosen in order to preserve information about rule symmetry.

This is probably the most reusable idea in the thesis.

In modern language, the construction is closely related to the influence/sensitivity of Boolean variables in a Boolean function. That observation should be checked carefully against the modern literature before claiming novelty or equivalence, but it provides an obvious mathematical vocabulary for reformulating the 1994 idea.

The thesis also distinguishes several related operations that should not be collapsed into one concept:

- removing genuinely unused neighborhood variables (embedding);
- removing anisotropic rules;
- restricting the rule space through an intermediate mapping, for example totalistic/additive descriptions;
- measuring table variation through Flip;
- measuring individual input influence through `E`.

These form a family of possible static structural filters rather than one filter.

## 6. The experiment around the parameter

The thesis does not establish a general classification theorem for `E`. It performs an exploratory calculation on a coherent restricted subspace of binary Moore-neighborhood rules: additive rules whose secondary table uses the values/functions `0`, `1`, and `U` (unchanged), yielding `3^10 = 59049` rules.

For this subspace it plots lambda together with embedding/influence values for the centre, the surrounding neighbors, and their mean, locating Anneal in the same space. It then reports attempts to correlate these static parameters with trajectories from random initial configurations of density 1/2 on finite tori.

This should be described as a **research programme and pilot experiment**, not as a demonstrated predictive classifier. The thesis itself ends the chapter by saying that the structure of CA rule space remains largely open and that neither the possible dynamical forms in large rule spaces nor a combinatorics of rules controlling their emergence is known.

That distinction matters for the present project: the 1994 work gives us a candidate coordinate system and reduction strategy, not evidence that it will by itself isolate class-IV or excitable rules.

## 7. Intermediate mappings and reduced rule families

One idea that deserves more attention than it received in our initial discussion is the use of **intermediate mappings**. Instead of enumerating arbitrary truth tables, the rule can depend on a reduced description of its neighborhood — for example a sum of neighbors — and the secondary table can contain constants or transformations such as Same/Unchanged and Flip.

This creates deliberately structured subspaces whose size can be chosen to match available computational resources while retaining properties such as isotropy.

This is directly continuous with the present idea of using totalistic/isotropic restrictions to make a 2D search feasible. Importantly, this idea is genuinely present in the DEA; it is not a retrospective import from the new project. Appendix C makes the motivation explicit: increase the number of states and vary the allowed transformation functions in the secondary table so that the resulting rule-space size is adapted to available resources and to the measurements being sought.

This may be as important to a modern research programme as the embedding parameter itself.

## 8. Dynamics as projected observables

The thesis repeatedly searches for low-dimensional observables of enormous trajectories. Examples include:

- population of bit planes;
- differences of populations between generations;
- XOR/differential reductions between successive configurations;
- accumulated reductions over time;
- texture statistics;
- plots of one state-variable population against another;
- space-time slices and projections;
- estimates of boundary size.

The motivation is not simply visualization. These are attempts to detect attractors, phase changes, scale behavior and coupling/decoupling between variables without retaining or inspecting the full state.

This is conceptually close to what the current project calls a **light dynamic filter**. Again, the modern pipeline should not be projected into the thesis, but the methodological continuity is real: use cheap observables to decide where expensive analysis is justified.

## 9. Anneal: what was actually observed

Chapter 4 is an experimental study rather than a consequence of the embedding work.

Anneal is studied on finite periodic 2D spaces, particularly from random initial conditions near density 1/2. The thesis reports unexpectedly long transients, large limit cycles, evolution of domain boundaries/islands, and trajectories tending toward near-homogeneous states. For increasing spatial sizes, normalized population/boundary curves are compared in search of a relative spatio-temporal invariance.

The strongest language in the thesis is deliberately tentative: it **proposes** the existence of a relative spatio-temporal invariance associated with a transition in configuration space. It does not demonstrate a scaling law.

Several concrete observations/questions survive the rereading:

- limit-cycle lengths can be surprisingly large and sometimes comparable to a spatial radius;
- transient duration grows dramatically in the reported experiments;
- density close to 1/2 appears important, and tiny deviations may influence which near-homogeneous state is reached;
- changes in the number and size of islands correlate with visible inflections in reduced observables;
- the thesis asks whether transient length itself can be treated as a property to search for in rule space.

The experimental data are interesting enough to reproduce, but the claims should now be tested statistically across ensembles of initial configurations and sizes rather than inferred from a handful of trajectories.

## 10. Intellectual strengths visible in the thesis

Reading the complete document changes the interpretation of its strengths and weaknesses.

The strongest aspect is not formal mathematics. It is the persistent identification of *spaces that are too large to inspect directly* and the invention of representations, reductions and parameterizations that make questions about them experimentally accessible.

Several intuitions recur:

- rule space has internal structure and should not be sampled as an undifferentiated set of integers;
- nominal neighborhood size differs from effective dependency;
- scalar summaries lose important structural information;
- symmetry and isotropy should be used deliberately to reduce search spaces;
- configuration trajectories require projections and observables;
- space and time can often be treated through common transformations;
- computational resource limits should shape the representation, not merely truncate the experiment;
- exhaustive analysis should be reserved for a reduced set selected by cheaper stages.

These intuitions are coherent even where the mathematical language is incomplete.

## 11. Limits and weaknesses

The principal scientific limitation is that many promising notions stop at the stage of construction or exploratory observation.

The embedding vector is defined and sampled, but its invariance properties, relation to known Boolean-function measures, statistical distribution and predictive relation to dynamics are not developed.

The Anneal experiments reveal phenomena but do not establish scaling laws or uncertainty estimates. Initial conditions are not sampled in a way that would support strong probabilistic conclusions.

The thesis moves frequently between engineering, epistemological analogy, visualization and dynamical conjecture. This breadth generates ideas but sometimes obscures which statements are definitions, implementation choices, empirical observations or hypotheses.

Some terminology is idiosyncratic. A modern reconstruction should translate concepts into standard mathematical language before deciding whether two apparently similar ideas are actually equivalent.

Finally, the bottom-up universal-workbench ambition is too broad. The conclusion recognizes this explicitly. A modern continuation should be much narrower and should build only the machinery required to answer specified questions.

## 12. Relationship to the later work and the current project

Three layers must remain distinct.

### Layer A — the 1994 DEA

Actually present in the thesis:

- table-based representation and manipulation of rules;
- structured/reduced rule families through intermediate mappings;
- Flip and per-neighbor embedding/influence measures;
- cheap reductions and projections of dynamics;
- exploratory correlation between rule parameters and observed behavior;
- Anneal experiments on long transients, cycles, boundaries and possible scaling/invariance.

### Layer B — later work

The attraction-basin work, especially quotienting finite configuration spaces by spatial translation, is later work. It must not be cited as a result of the DEA.

Nevertheless it is intellectually continuous with the DEA: both attack combinatorial explosion by identifying equivalences or irrelevant dimensions before doing expensive computation.

### Layer C — the proposed restart

The current three-stage idea can now be stated without anachronism:

1. **static structural reduction** using dependency/influence, symmetry and deliberately restricted rule representations;
2. **cheap dynamical characterization** on sampled finite spaces;
3. **expensive exhaustive basin analysis modulo spatial symmetries** only for selected rules.

The DEA supports the rationale for stages 1 and 2. Stage 3 comes from later work.

## 13. What should be reformulated mathematically before coding

Before reviving the old C implementation, the most useful next step is probably a mathematical normalization of the static concepts.

Questions to settle include:

1. Express `E` precisely as Boolean-variable influence / discrete derivative and determine under which probability measure the 1994 formula operates (apparently uniform over truth-table inputs).
2. Generalize from binary states to multi-state cells without prematurely collapsing the result to a scalar.
3. Separate **dependency**, **anisotropy**, **symmetry class**, and **effective neighborhood** as distinct invariants/features.
4. Determine behavior under permutations of neighborhood positions, relabeling of states, reflection/rotation and complementation.
5. Characterize the exact subspace corresponding to rules embedded from smaller neighborhoods.
6. Compare Flip with standard notions such as total variation along a chosen truth-table ordering; determine how much of Flip is an artifact of the encoding/order of table entries.
7. Treat the influence vector, its distribution and symmetry-derived summaries as features rather than assume that its mean is the correct control parameter.
8. Formalize intermediate mappings (totalistic, outer-totalistic, isotropic, etc.) as maps/quotients or parameterized rule families.

This work would tell us which parts of the 1994 static sieve are mathematically intrinsic and which depend on representation choices.

## 14. What should be reproduced experimentally

A minimal reproducibility programme could proceed independently of the old GUI/workbench:

- reproduce the 59,049-rule additive `01U` experiment;
- verify the exact 1994 `E` and Flip calculations against the old code/data;
- compute modern feature vectors for the same rules;
- reproduce representative Anneal trajectories at the documented sizes and densities;
- repeat them over ensembles with controlled density offsets and random seeds;
- measure transient length, cycle length, density, boundary size and simple spatial correlation statistics;
- test finite-size scaling claims rather than infer them visually;
- only then connect selected rules to the later quotient-basin machinery.

This would separate historical reconstruction from new research while using the former as a source of test cases.

## 15. Assessment of the original ambitions and limits

The thesis does show the limits described in our conversation: the mathematical formalization is often incomplete, and some hypotheses are carried farther by computational intuition than by proof. But it would be misleading to describe the work simply as intuition blocked by lack of mathematical education.

There is a recognizable research strategy: identify combinatorial explosion, construct a representation, find reductions that preserve a chosen aspect of structure, build an experiment, and inspect whether the reduced coordinates expose regularities. That strategy is applied repeatedly to rules, neighborhoods, trajectories and visualization.

The limitation is more specific: the work lacks a sufficiently developed mathematical framework for deciding which reductions are invariant, canonical or theoretically informative, and it lacks enough computational/statistical power to validate many of the empirical conjectures. Those are precisely areas where a modern collaboration can add value without rewriting the intellectual history of the project.

## 16. Recommended next research discussion

The best starting point for a new research phase is **not** to modernize all of the 1994 software.

The first question should be narrower:

> What is the mathematically intrinsic version of the 1994 embedding/influence idea, and how much reduction of the proposed modern rule spaces does it actually buy us before any simulation?

From there, the natural sequence is:

- formalize static invariants/features;
- quantify their distributions over the chosen modern rule family;
- design a cheap dynamic feature set;
- determine empirically whether static and cheap dynamic features enrich for the behaviors of interest;
- reserve quotient-basin construction for the resulting candidates.

This preserves the deepest continuity with the DEA while avoiding its main weakness: building a very general environment before the scientific question has been narrowed enough.
